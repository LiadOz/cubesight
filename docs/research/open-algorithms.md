# Open algorithm sources and self-generated algorithms

Status: research and prototype. Not legal advice. Nothing in `src/` or `tests/` was touched; all code is under `docs/research/alg-gen/` (node scripts, no dependencies beyond the repo's own `cubing` and the existing xcross WASM). Prototype outputs are in `docs/research/alg-gen/out/`.

## 1. Summary and recommendation

1. **No open, clearly licensed OLL/PLL/F2L algorithm database exists** that is both complete and safe to bundle. The big collections (SpeedSolving wiki, SpeedCubeDB, J Perm, AlgDb-derived sets, CubeSkills, Ruwix) either state no licence at all, state "all rights reserved", or are CC BY-SA/GPL (copyleft). The MIT-licensed trainers on GitHub (Alg-Trainer, cubedex, cubefsrs) license their *code*; the alg lists inside them are copied from the big collections with no licence of their own.
2. **Recommended base: self-generated algorithms.** A node script (`alg-gen/`) searches the cube group for short, finger-friendly algorithms per case and writes a bundled JSON at release. Every alg is generated by our own search and verified two independent ways, so it has no third-party rights attached. It reproduces the classic algs (Sune, Ua/Ub, Jb, Rb, T, H, Z, Ga-Gd, Aa/Ab) where depth allows, so users still see algs they recognise.
3. **Case identification needs no external data.** The 57 OLL and 21 PLL cases are derived from cube state (`alg-gen/ll-classes.mjs` reproduces 57 and 21 from the 62,208-state last-layer group). Only the *numbering* (OLL 27 = Sune) and *names* (T-perm) are conventions; they are facts/labels, not an alg collection.
4. **F2L: do not ship a case table at all.** The "best pair completion from here" prototype (section 5) searches the live position directly, matches the existing WASM engine's lengths on 108/108 comparisons, and runs in tens of ms median (with tables) to a few hundred ms (without). It covers pairs 1-4, pseudo slots, multislotting and keyholes with one mechanism.
5. **Optionally add** CC0/MIT/CC BY material with credit if any appears (none found today that is worth the provenance risk), and **link out** (do not copy) to SpeedSolving wiki / SpeedCubeDB / J Perm for "more algs for this case".
6. **Flag:** `src/pll-logic.js` already contains 21 hand-typed PLL algs whose comment says they were "cross-checked against ... CubeSkills and CubingApp". Short sequences are low risk, but the provenance is exactly the kind the user wants to avoid; the generated set can replace them.

Notes on what the licences imply:

- **CC BY-SA 4.0** (Wikibooks/Wikipedia): attribution required, adapted material must be released under the same or a compatible licence. Whether a bundled alg table inside an app is an "adaptation" or a "collection" is arguable; for a proprietary app the safe reading is "don't".
- **GPL-3.0** (csTimer and any GPL trainer): data that is part of the GPL work is covered. Copying it into a non-GPL app and distributing it breaches the licence. A fully GPL-3.0 CubeSight would be the only compatible option.
- **MIT trainers**: the MIT notice only covers what the copyright holder owns. If their lists were copied from SpeedCubeDB/SSW, MIT cannot launder that; the repos do not state provenance, so they fail the "nothing with unclear rights" test.
- **SpeedSolving wiki contributions**: authors retain rights; the site only obtained a licence to itself. Absence of a public licence means default all-rights-reserved for the wiki's creative selection/text, whatever the status of the bare move sequences.

### Legal status of algorithms (general background, not legal advice)

- **Individual algs as short move sequences** (e.g. `R U R' U R U2 R'`): in common-law and civil-law systems these are very likely not protectable as a literary work: they are a functional method/procedure (US 17 U.S.C. 102(b): no copyright in a "procedure, process, system, method of operation"), they are extremely short, they are largely dictated by the puzzle (merger of idea and expression), and many are independently re-discovered. Authorship credits (e.g. "Sune", named after a person; "J Perm" algs) are conventions of credit, not rights. Practical risk is low, but it is not zero, and it is unrelated to the user's requirement of clear provenance.
- **Compiled databases**: a curated collection can have **thin copyright in selection and arrangement** (US: Feist; EU: Database Directive 96/9/EC art. 3 "own intellectual creation"), and in the **EU a sui generis database right** (art. 7, 15 years, renewable on substantial new investment) protects against extraction/re-utilisation of a *substantial part* of a database where there was substantial investment in **obtaining, verifying or presenting** the contents (CJEU, e.g. *British Horseracing Board v William Hill*, *Fixtures Marketing*: investment in *creating* the data does not count). A community-maintained alg DB with curation, ratings and verification may qualify; the US has no equivalent right. Scraping a site can additionally breach its terms of use (contract), independent of copyright.
- **Consequence**: copying a whole table (even of unprotectable sequences) from SpeedCubeDB/SSW/J Perm is the risky act (extraction of a substantial part + selection/arrangement). Generating our own algs, or using a handful of universally-known algs from first principles, avoids both. Re-typing "the" published list is still extraction in spirit; we do not recommend it.
- **Names and numbers** ("OLL 27", "T-perm", "Sune") are naming conventions and short labels; using them is ordinary practice. Open question: confirm there is no objection to using the conventional 1-57 numbering table (it is a fact mapping, but it is itself published in the collections).

## 3. Part 2: self-generated algorithms

### What exists in the repo

- `cubing` **0.63.7**, licence `MPL-2.0 OR GPL-3.0-or-later`. It runs in node 22 (`import ... from '.../cubing/puzzles/index.js'`). `experimentalSolve3x3x3IgnoringCenters(pattern)` works in node (first call about 150 ms for init, then ~1 ms) but it is a **full-cube two-phase solver**: it solves the entire cube with all 18 moves; it cannot restrict to LL algs, to a generator set, or preserve F2L, and its output is not ergonomic (e.g. T-perm came back as `U F2 U' F2 D R2 B2 U B2 D' R2`). So it is not suited to generating algs; KPuzzle (`kpuzzle.algToTransformation`) is useful and is used here as the move-table source and as the **independent verifier**.
- `src/cross-cube.js` is an object/sticker model (position vectors) - fine for rendering and checks but slow for search; the generator uses compact typed-array permutation/orientation states built from KPuzzle.
- `src/pll-logic.js` `PLL_CASES` provides the 21 case definitions (the reference alg only *defines which case* it is; they are never shipped as the answer).
- WASM: `src/xcross-wasm` (cube-xcross, MIT, commit ddeea84...) is a cross/xcross/xxcross solver driven by a slot mask. It is the right tool for pairs 1-2 but see section 5 for pairs 3-4.

### Prototype (files in `docs/research/alg-gen/`)

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `cube.mjs` | Cubie model from KPuzzle move tables; compose/invert/hash; notation helpers (invert, mirror, STM/ETM, ergo score) |
| `search.mjs` | Meet-in-the-middle search over a generator set; A-half tabulated by an F2L-slot key, B-half enumerated as the inverse and probed; every candidate checked exactly |
| `cases.mjs` | PLL cases (from `src/pll-logic.js`) and 16 OLL cases; OLL goal = F2L solved + LL oriented (any permutation) |
| `run.mjs` | Driver: runs all generator sets per case, dedupes, ranks, flags whether the classic alg was rediscovered |
| `verify.mjs` | Re-checks every generated alg with cubing.js KPuzzle directly (separate code path) |
| `report.mjs`, `ll-classes.mjs` | Tables for this doc; derivation of 57/21 classes |
| `pairbest.mjs`, `bench-pairs.mjs` | "Best pair completion" engine and benchmark (section 5) |

Method: for case effect E, find X (no leading/trailing U: those are reported as pre/post AUF) such that `scramble(E)` then X is solved (PLL: up to a final U turn; OLL: F2L solved and LL oriented). Centres are part of the state, so algs that leave a net rotation are rejected (H/Z with M slices are fine). One move per face per axis run in a fixed order removes commuting duplicates (`L R` vs `R L`). Generator sets: `<R,U>`, `<R,U,F>`, `<R,U,D>`, `<R,U,L>`, `<R,U,F,D>`, `<R,U,r>`, `<R,U,M>` (with the two halves up to 8+8, 7+7, 7+7, 7+7, 6+6, 6+7, 6+7 moves; RUF/RUD also 7+8 in a deep run).

Ergonomic score (lower is better): STM + per-move penalty (F 1, D 1, L 1.5, B 2, r/M 0.5) + 1 per regrip (a run of F/B/D/L moves) + 1 per pre/post AUF turn. ETM is also reported (M counts 2). This is deliberately simple; it ranks `R U R' U R U2 R'` first for Sune and puts F/D-heavy algs behind `<R,U>` ones.

**Verification:** 442 PLL + 160 `<R,U>` deep + 628 OLL generated algs were re-verified with cubing.js KPuzzle: **0 failures** (`out/verify-*.log`).

### Rediscovery of the well-known algorithms

Classic algs found exactly (core without AUF, as listed by the app's reference where in depth): **Sune, Anti-Sune, OLL 21 H, OLL 22 Pi, OLL 45/33/37/35/28/51/1/43/34; PLL H, Ua, Ub, Z, T (`R U R' U' R' F R2 U' R' U' R U R' F'`), Jb (`R U R' F' R U R' U' R' F R2 U' R'` + U'), Rb, Ra, Aa, Ab, Ga, Gb, Gc, Gd.** Not rediscovered only because the classic is outside the searched depth or sets: E (17 STM), F (18), Na (16), Nb (17), V (16), Y (16), Ja (the classic is 13 STM over `<U,F,L>`, a combination I did not search), OLL 57 (the classic mixes `M'` and `r`, which no single generator set contains), and OLL 44/OLL 2 whose reference uses the wide `f` (an S-slice move not in any generator set). The search often finds alternatives that are as short or shorter (e.g. Jb in 10 STM with `<R,U,L>`: `L R U2 R' U' R U2 L' U R'`; Ua/Ub in 7 STM with M slices).

Interesting findings: `<R,U>` alone only yields Ua, Ub, H and Z up to 20 moves (`out/pll-ru20.json`); the other PLLs need F, D or L. Several PLLs have only half-turn-heavy 14-15 STM algs in the sets searched (E, F, Na, V, Y), which are not what people actually use; for those, the generated data needs a deeper/larger set or more weight on known-good shapes (open question).

### Inverse and mirror dedupe

- `invertAlg(X)` solves the inverse case; `mirrorAlg(X)` solves the mirror case. The tables below list each PLL case's inverse and mirror case (computed from state, up to AUF): Aa<->Ab (inverse and mirror), Ua<->Ub, Ga/Gb/Gc/Gd form a ring, Ja<->Jb (mirror), Ra<->Rb (mirror), Na<->Nb (mirror), and E, F, H, T, V, Y, Z, Ja, Jb, Na, Nb, Ra, Rb are self-inverse as cases.
- So only about half the cases need searching; the rest are derived by mirroring (swap R/L, flip direction) or inverting a found alg. In the prototype, aliases within a case (an alg and its inverse for self-inverse cases) are kept as separate entries only if both were found; the build step should add the derived ones and drop duplicates by (canonical string).
- Regrip-quality can differ between an alg and its mirror (right-hand dominant), so derived algs are re-scored.

| OLL 34 Fish-ish | 40 | `R U R2 U' R' F R U R U' F'` (11 STM, 15) | 11 STM: `R U R2 U' R' F R U R U' F'` | yes |


Case numbering follows my recollection of the common 1-57 numbering and is **unverified**; only the names/labels depend on it. The orientation patterns themselves are derived from state, so the generator can cover all 57 once a numbering table is agreed. `OLL 44`/`OLL 2` show "no" only because their reference alg uses wide `f`, not because the case was not solved (the generator found 6- and 11-move algs for them).

### Build-time versus runtime generation

Generation is cheap in node (about 1.5 s per case for all sets, under a few minutes for all 78 LL cases at the depths above), but running it in the browser means shipping the search code, 300-400k-entry tables per set (a few MB transient) and 1-2 s of main-thread-blocking work per case on a phone (JS is about 2-4x slower than this machine). It would also make the output nondeterministic across devices if time-boxed.

**Proposal:** build-time script (`docs/research/alg-gen/run.mjs`, productionised under `scripts/`) run at release, writing a bundled JSON (about 80 cases x 5-10 algs, roughly 30-60 KB before compression), checked into the repo and re-verified by a unit test with the app's own `cross-cube.js` model. Runtime does only: state -> case id (table lookup), and move-count/ergo scoring of the user's actual moves against the table. Optional later: an on-demand worker search (the 20-500 ms variants) for drills with custom generator sets.

## 4. How case IDs and "better alg" suggestions would use it

- **Case ID**: from the cube state at the start of OLL/PLL (cross + F2L solved), compute the LL piece permutation/orientation; canonicalise under the AUF group (the app already does this for PLL in `canonicalPllSignature`). OLL: orientation pattern up to y rotation (216 patterns -> 58 classes incl. solved; verified in `ll-classes.mjs`). PLL: 22 classes incl. solved (verified). The signature maps to a case record `{id, name, algs[]}` in the bundled JSON. Two-look OLL/PLL: first look = edge-orientation class (dot/L/line/solved) then corner case; PLL first look corners-then-edges. These are derived from the same state, no data needed beyond which sub-case an alg achieves.
- **"Better algorithm / AUF" suggestions**: compare the user's played moves for the stage against the case's alg list: (a) if the user's moves, normalised (AUF stripped, commuting moves ordered), match a known alg, report which one; (b) otherwise, verify the user's moves solve the case (state check), and suggest the top-ranked alg with fewer STM or a lower ergo score, and show the pre/post-AUF needed. AUF suggestions fall out of the search results: each stored alg lists the AUFs it needs, so the app can recommend "use pre-AUF U' with this alg to save one move" by trying all 16 AUF combinations (cheap table lookups).
- **Drills**: scramble generation is the inverse of any stored alg for the case (setup is the reference alg inverted; random pre-AUF); the app already does this in `generatePllCase`. The multiple algs per case allow "learn this one" selection and mirror/inverse variants.
- **F2L pairs 3-4**: see section 5; no case tables are needed.

## 5. F2L: best pair completion from here

Prototype: `pairbest.mjs` (engine) and `bench-pairs.mjs` (benchmark).

**Design.** Position = cross plus any solved slots (cross solved in the plain frame; a pseudo/offset cross is handled by letting D turns be ordinary moves, so a final D is just a move and the "pseudo" flag is "last move is D"). Goal for a candidate = cross edges plus all already-solved pairs plus the new pair (or two new pairs for multislot) at home. Search = IDA* over all 18 face turns on a compact tracked state of 12 pieces (4 cross edges, 4 slot edges, 4 slot corners, each a single byte `slot*orientations+orientation`), with moves as byte lookup tables. Pruning heuristic = max of: (a) cross-edges distance table (24^4 = 332k bytes), (b) per-slot pair table (corner+edge, 576 states), (c) optional cross+slot-edge tables (24^5 = 8M bytes per slot, 4 slots = 32 MB) built by BFS. Every unsolved slot is searched, any pair of unsolved slots (multislot), and results within +1 move of the shortest are collected and ranked by the ergo score (and by the planner's `weightedMoveCount`). Keyholes and multislot insertions appear automatically because the goal is only "pieces home", not a fixed algorithm shape.

**Correctness:** 40 random solves x (cross, pairs 1-4); every chosen solution re-verified by cubing.js KPuzzle (0 failures). Against the existing cube-xcross WASM with mask = already-solved slots + candidate slot: **108 slot queries, all proven by WASM, lengths equal in 108/108** (pair 1: 48/48, pair 2: 36/36, pair 3: 24/24).

**Runtime (node 22, 40 positions; "all open slots" = a full ranked answer):**

| Stage | With the 32 MB cross+edge tables | Without them (cross + pair tables only, 332 KB) |
|---|---|---|
| Table build | 5.8 s once (JS BFS; fine at build time, 32 MB is too big to ship, see below) | 70 ms |
| Pair 1, all 4 slots | median 88 ms, p95 170 ms, max 183 ms | median 269 ms, p95 632 ms |
| Pair 2, 3 slots | median 76 ms, p95 312 ms, max 404 ms | median 318 ms, p95 1.1 s |
| Pair 3, 2 slots | median 69 ms, p95 488 ms, max 1.6 s | median 282 ms, p95 1.8 s, max 2.3 s |
| Pair 4, 1 slot | median 51 ms, p95 1.0 s, max 4.2 s | median 194 ms; **2/40 hit the 10 s cap with +1 slack** (shortest-only is much faster) |
| Multislot (every pair of open slots, shortest only), with tables | from the pair-1 position: median 1.6 s, p95 4.2 s, max 12.9 s | from pair-2: 1.3 s / 4.2 s; from pair-3 (last two slots): 0.6 s / 5.6 s (max 10 s cap hit once) |

Shortest completion lengths over the benchmark: pair 1 mean 5.1 (max 6), pair 2 5.6 (7), pair 3 6.0 (8), pair 4 6.6 (9).

For comparison, the WASM engine: pair 1 median 19 ms (max 683), pair 2 median 52 ms (max 741), pair 3 median 15 ms (max 896) for single-slot queries on the same positions; but for 3-4 slots from scratch it is slow: from a cross-only position, `mask=3` (two slots) took 1.7-15 s, `mask=7` (three slots) timed out at 20 s, and from a position with two solved slots `mask=7` (+1 slot) took 1.1 s while `mask=15` (all four, i.e. pair 4) timed out at 15 s with depth 10. So the WASM engine handles pairs 1-3 acceptably when the solved slots are in the mask, but pair 4 and any multislot/two-new-pair goals are out of its comfortable range.

**Comparison with `src/f2l-planner.js` / `src/analysis/pairs.js`.** `plannerChoices` does not search: it filters and ranks results the WASM produced (verify, preserve solved pairs, cheapest by `weightedMoveCount`, pseudo = last move D). `pairs.js` runs `SINGLE_MASKS` or `PAIR_MASKS` (existing slot + one) and explicitly stops at `PAIR_ENGINE_PAIRS = 2`; pseudo frames are marked unsupported. The prototype replaces the search part: same ranking input (a list of verified move lists) but with all four pairs, multislot, and pseudo from one engine. The planner's weight (F/B = 5) can be reused unchanged on its output (the prototype returns `w` using that weight and `e` using the ergo score).

**Feasibility of pairs 3-4:**
- *Same WASM engine*: pair 3 yes (single new slot, solved slots in the mask: ~1 s worst seen); pair 4 and multislot: no (timeouts).
- *New IDA\* (this prototype)*: yes. Pairs 3-4 and multislot all work; pair 3/4 typical answers in under 0.5 s without the big tables, and the compact tables (332 KB + 576 entries) are tiny to ship. Worst cases (pair 4, +1 slack) hit seconds, so run it in the existing cross-solver Web Worker with the same time-budget/abort pattern, show the shortest first and refine.
- *Precomputed F2L case tables*: not needed; a table keyed by slot-relative state (corner position x orientation x edge position x orientation = 576 raw states, 41 standard cases up to slot symmetry) would give instant case ID/optimal first-insertion length for a pair in isolation, but cannot account for the other three slots and the cross interacting; useful only as the heuristic (which is what table (b) is).
- The big win in speed would be a symmetry-reduced cross+edge table (use the y-rotation symmetry to store one slot instead of four, 8 MB raw, compressible, or 4-bit packed to 4 MB); this removes the 3x slowdown without shipping 32 MB.

**Live use.** Pairs 1-3: with the bigger tables median <100 ms and p95 <0.5 s on a desktop; without them 0.3 s median and 1-2 s p95, so a worker + progressive display works live; extrapolate 2-4x slower on phones. Pair 4: single slot, shortest-only is fast; slack search can take seconds, so keep it for the review screen, not live.

## 6. Open questions

1. **Numbering**: OK to use the conventional OLL 1-57 numbering (and names like Sune, T-perm)? It must be supplied as data; I can anchor it to reference algs verified by state but the numbering table itself is a convention published in the big collections.
2. **Which ergonomic score?** The current heuristic is crude (no hand model, no per-finger tuning, no "flick vs wrist" distinction). Do we want known-good shape weighting, or user-rated favourites later (stored locally)?
3. **Classic algs that aren't short in the searched sets** (E, F, Na, V, Y; Ga-Gd): do we want the build to run deeper (RUFD 7+7, RUD 8+8) or accept the more half-turn-heavy results the search found? Algorithms people actually drill matter for drills even if not shortest.
4. **Wide/slice moves**: `f`/`S`, `l`, `u`, `d`, and rotations (x, y, z) are not in the generator sets; many popular OLL algs need `f`. Add `RUf`? Output notation also needs a decision (with `M`/rotations or `L`/`D` equivalents).
5. **Existing PLL algs in `src/pll-logic.js`** have a stated provenance (CubeSkills/CubingApp cross-check). Replace with generated ones, or keep (short sequences, but clear-rights goal)?
6. **GPL option**: if the whole app were GPL-3.0, csTimer and GPL trainer data could be reused; is that a direction at all?
7. **Pseudo-slot semantics**: the prototype treats pseudo as "a D move is allowed anywhere"; the planner currently labels pseudo only when the last move is D. Align definitions before replacing `plannerChoices`.
8. **Cross not solved yet / pseudo cross at start**: prototype requires a plain cross at the start; a pseudo-cross start needs an initial D^k frame (cheap to add).
9. **Contacting authors**: for any single curated list we do want (e.g. a recognised F2L set), ask SpeedCubeDB/J Perm/SSW for explicit permission; a written CC0/CC BY grant would settle it.
